# 照片 × 木刻版畫雙層海報 Prompt

> 收藏日期：2026-10-14
> 來源：本次上傳的「貼上的文字.txt」；原作者與來源網址未提供
> 用途：每張照片各自製作一張 3:4 直式海報，上半保留攝影，下半轉成柔亮限色木刻／膠版畫
> 適用工具：支援上傳照片與圖像編輯的圖像生成模型；本次未測試生成效果

## 使用方式

1. 上傳一張或多張照片，貼入下方完整英文 Prompt。
2. 每張照片獨立輸出，上下各占總高度 50%。
3. 下半採手工版畫質感、暖紙留白與不完整印邊，標題依照片生成。

## Prompt 全文

```text
Create a separate, independent premium design poster from each photo I upload. Do not combine multiple photos; output each photo individually. Use a vertical 3:4 composition, with the upper and lower sections strictly divided 1:1, each occupying exactly 50% of the total image height. In the upper half, preserve the original photograph, including the subject’s identity, structure, pose, spatial relationships, realistic materials, natural lighting and shadows, and original color atmosphere. Apply only subtle and refined photographic color grading to give it the quality of an independent magazine, art publication, or exhibition photograph. To fit the format, naturally extend the sky, ground, or surrounding environment when necessary, but never stretch, distort, mirror, redraw, replace, or alter the main subject. In the lower half, identify and extract only the most recognizable subjects, silhouettes, poses, gestures, objects, structural relationships, and narrative connections from the original photograph, then reinterpret them as a limited-color woodcut or linocut print impression floating on warm paper. Do not simply copy the entire photograph, trace every object, or apply a generic filter. Actively remove complex backgrounds, visual clutter, and unnecessary details, keeping only the contours, proportions, direction, light-and-dark divisions, key structures, gestures, spatial relationships, and visual memory points that make the original image immediately recognizable. Reconstruct these essential elements through the visual language of woodcut, linocut, and handmade multi-color block printing. The result should remain clearly connected to the original photograph while feeling simplified, slightly awkward, tactile, handmade, and intentionally imperfect. Linework should not be overly smooth or precise. Allow visible carving marks, uneven line thickness, hesitant cuts, rough edges, abrupt turns, small breaks, gaps, irregular negative space, slight misregistration, worn areas, dry-brush texture, incomplete printing, missing ink, and coarse grain. Avoid polished vector illustration, clean digital outlines, glossy rendering, or cartoon styling. The image should feel as if it were physically carved by hand and printed layer by layer with real ink. Build the lower composition around one strong visual anchor, generous negative space, and incomplete edges. Let the position and scale of the subject follow the original visual weight, gesture, movement, structure, and directional flow. The subject may be centered, offset, lightly cropped, or extended toward one side when appropriate. Preserve large areas of warm paper around the print, and allow the image edges to gradually disappear through broken ink, exposed paper, worn texture, and irregular printing rather than forming a complete rectangular frame. The final impression should feel like a memory gently pressed onto paper. Extract the most recognizable and emotionally meaningful colors from the upper photograph, then reinterpret them using a fresh, soft, comforting, bright limited-color palette. Suitable colors include cream white, ivory, pale pink, mist green, light blue, soft yellow, pale orange, mint green, gentle coral, light ochre, muted teal, and other clean, airy tones. Maintain low-to-medium saturation, high brightness, and soft contrast. Preserve the strongest color memory from the original image while avoiding dark, muddy, dull, heavy, overly distressed, or excessively vintage color schemes. All colors should feel like matte physical inks printed in separate layers, slightly translucent and imperfect, while retaining visible grain and the warmth of traditional printmaking. Typography should become part of the visual composition rather than functioning as fixed explanatory text. Based on the subject, action, emotional tone, relationship, location, atmosphere, or metaphor within the photograph, automatically generate a short 1–3 word main title together with a very small set of keywords or micro-notes. The main title may follow the subject’s contour, negative-space boundary, visual axis, or edge of the printed image, interacting naturally with the composition through support, overlap, offset, alignment, or visual echo. Keep supporting text small, restrained, and editorial, using typewriter-style, monospaced serif, archival, or understated print typography, similar to annotations in a travel notebook, private print collection, or independent art publication. Do not automatically add a year or fixed information; let all text grow naturally from the content of the photograph. The overall result should emphasize handmade woodcut character, fresh and gentle limited colors, generous paper space, incomplete printed edges, subtle grain, tactile ink texture, and restrained editorial typography. The mood should feel warm, quiet, comforting, light, nostalgic, and emotionally grounded without becoming dark or overly retro. Whether the subject is a person, family interaction, animal, plant, building, street scene, market, food stall, household object, vehicle, interior, landscape, or everyday moment, maintain a clear and thoughtful visual relationship between the realistic photograph above and the reconstructed print below. Prioritize emotional memory, visual recognition, gesture, relationship, and atmosphere over literal reproduction. Avoid generic filters, full-background replication, polished vector outlines, cartoon illustration, 3D rendering, overly detailed tracing, formulaic travel-poster styling, heavy grunge, and cheap retro effects.
```
